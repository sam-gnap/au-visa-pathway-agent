What counts as "regional Australia"

For skilled and graduate visa purposes, "regional Australia" is defined by Home Affairs as all of Australia except the major metropolitan areas of Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane. The definition is broader than many applicants expect: Perth, Adelaide, the Gold Coast, the Sunshine Coast, Newcastle, Wollongong, Canberra, and Hobart are all classified as regional for skilled migration purposes.

Regional postcodes are split into two categories used by different visa programs.

Category 2 - cities and major regional centres. Covers Perth, Adelaide, Gold Coast, Sunshine Coast, Canberra, Newcastle/Lake Macquarie, Wollongong/Illawarra, Geelong, and Hobart. Used by some 491 and 494 nominations.

Category 3 - regional centres and other regional areas. Covers everywhere else outside the Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane metropolitan areas, including all of the Northern Territory, all of Tasmania outside Hobart, and most of regional Queensland, NSW, Victoria, SA, and WA.

Why it matters:

- Subclass 491 (Skilled Work Regional) requires applicants to live, work, and study only in a designated regional area for the full 5-year provisional period.
- Subclass 485 graduates of regional universities may be eligible for an extra 1 or 2 years on the visa.
- 491 holders who meet income and residence requirements for at least 3 years can apply for the 191 permanent residence pathway.
- State nomination for the 190 and 491 typically requires demonstrated commitment to the state and, for 491, regional residence.
