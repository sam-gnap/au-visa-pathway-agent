Employer-sponsored work visas (subclasses 482 and 186)

Australia's main employer-sponsored work visas are the temporary Skills in Demand visa (subclass 482, formerly Temporary Skill Shortage) and the permanent Employer Nomination Scheme (subclass 186).

Subclass 482 - Skills in Demand. Temporary, up to 4 years. Three streams from late 2024:

- Specialist Skills: high-earning roles above a defined income threshold. Faster processing.
- Core Skills: occupations on the Core Skills Occupation List (CSOL). Replaces the former Short-Term and Medium-Term streams.
- Labour Agreement: roles covered by a tailored labour agreement between the employer and government.

Common 482 requirements: nomination by an approved sponsor, occupation on the relevant list, at least 1 year of relevant work experience in the last 5 years, salary at or above the Temporary Skilled Migration Income Threshold (currently AUD 73,150), and Vocational or Competent English depending on the stream.

Subclass 186 - Employer Nomination Scheme. Permanent. Three streams:

- Direct Entry: applicant has not previously held a relevant employer-sponsored visa. Requires a positive skills assessment and at least 3 years of relevant experience.
- Temporary Residence Transition: 482 (or legacy 457) holder who has worked for the sponsoring employer in the nominated occupation for at least 2 years out of the previous 3.
- Labour Agreement: same labour agreement framework as the 482.

ANZSCO requirements: both visas tie eligibility to the ANZSCO occupation code on the nomination. Duties and qualifications must match the ANZSCO description for that occupation. The sponsoring employer must demonstrate a genuine need.
