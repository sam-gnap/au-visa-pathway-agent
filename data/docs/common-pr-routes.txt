Common real-world routes to Australian permanent residence

How people actually get from a first visa to permanent residence, based on
recent invitation-round results and the routes migration practitioners most
commonly describe. Points, thresholds, and cutoffs move every program year —
treat the numbers below as indicative of 2025-26.

1. The points reality for Skilled Independent (189)

Meeting the 65-point minimum makes you eligible to lodge an expression of
interest, not to be invited. Invitation rounds now run quarterly, and recent
rounds cleared at very different levels depending on occupation: building
trades (electricians, carpenters, plumbers) around 65 points; registered
nurses, GPs, and secondary teachers around 75; engineering specialties around
85; and general professional and ICT occupations at 90 points or higher. An
offshore professional with 65-70 points is very unlikely to receive a 189
invitation and should treat state nomination (190/491) or employer
sponsorship (482) as the realistic route instead.

2. Working holiday to sponsorship (417/462 -> 482 -> 186)

The most common route for working-holiday makers who decide to stay: find an
employer willing to sponsor while working on the 417 or 462, move to the
Skills in Demand (482) visa, then transition to the Employer Nomination
Scheme (186) after about two years with the same sponsor. Two practical
constraints dominate this route. First, working-holiday makers generally
cannot work more than 6 months for a single employer, which limits the time
available to prove yourself before nomination. Second, the sponsoring role
must pay at least the Core Skills Income Threshold (AUD 76,515 in 2025-26,
rising to AUD 79,499 from 1 July 2026) and at least the market rate for the
role, and the occupation must be on the Core Skills Occupation List.

3. Study to sponsorship (500 -> 485 -> 482 -> 186)

The standard graduate route: finish an eligible Australian qualification,
take the Temporary Graduate (485) visa (2-6 years depending on qualification
level), use that time to build skilled experience in the nominated
occupation, then move to employer sponsorship (482) and later the 186.
Practitioners consistently warn that time on the 485 spent in unskilled
"survival jobs" does not build the evidence needed for the sponsored stage —
skilled, occupation-relevant employment is what makes the later nomination
credible. Graduates who accumulate points can also try 190/491 state
nomination in parallel.

4. Regional nomination (491 -> 191)

For applicants willing to live and work in regional Australia, state or
territory nomination for the 491 is often achievable at points levels where a
189 invitation is not, because states select against their own occupation
shortages. The 491 leads to the Permanent Residence (Skilled Regional) 191
after three years of meeting income and regional-residence requirements. The
equivalent city-oriented route is the 190, which is permanent on grant.

5. Direct employer nomination (186)

Experienced workers (usually 3+ years in the occupation) with an Australian
employer willing to nominate can go straight to the 186 Direct Entry stream
without a temporary stage, subject to the age limit (under 45 at
application, with limited exemptions), a skills assessment, and Competent
English.
